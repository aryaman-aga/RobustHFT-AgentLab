\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
\rhal{} defends an LLM trading agent against misinformation by grounding its reading of the news in market evidence that the attacker cannot edit. Over \val{NseedsRun} paired simulated markets, temporal grounding cuts misinformation-driven attack success by \cmp{CmpGainAdaptiveGroundedNet}, from \val{CmpNetNone}\% to \val{CmpNetGrounded}\%, for \val{CmpCostGrounded}\pp{} of clean-market return. It beats detection filtering, which a screening attacker evades, and robust aggregation, which cannot cope with a majority of injected documents. The gain holds with a local LLM reading the news, on real BTC-USD prices, against an attacker that aligns its edits with the market, and when the misinformation moves prices. A hold-on-conflict rule backfires because the attacker controls the conflict it reacts to, and shrinking the trade instead removes the penalty. Placebo controls, shared attacker randomness and frozen replays make these comparisons fair. Next steps are frontier LLMs, real news with an LLM-based attacker, and defenses against attackers that maximize text--market conflict.
