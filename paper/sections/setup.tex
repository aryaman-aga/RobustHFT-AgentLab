\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Data.} Each seed generates \val{NDays} business days of synthetic OHLCV from a three-regime Markov drift with GARCH(1,1) volatility~\cite{bollerslev1986garch}, and \val{NewsPerDay} template news items per day about a fictitious company. The items' latent sentiment correlates ($\rho=0.35$) with the next day's return, so news is genuinely but noisily informative. Every template contains an entity, a figure, a cause and a period, so each edit type has a target. The last \val{NevalDays} days are evaluated, matching AutoRedTrader's 60-day window, and the rest serve as warm-up and analog library.

\textbf{Configuration.} Retrieval is TF-IDF top-\val{TopK} over the last three days with recency decay. MisGen uses bias probability $0.7$, $\alpha=1$, $K=3$ attempts, $\tau_{\mathrm{sim}}=0.35$ and one candidate per clean item. Trades pay \val{FeeBps}\,bp fees and \val{SlipBps}\,bp slippage, long-only and without leverage.

\textbf{Arms.} Per seed and defense level (none; grounding with microstructure proxies; grounding plus risk layer) we run a clean arm, the adaptive attack, its frozen replay, and the three placebo controls: 18 paired arms per seed over \val{NseedsRun} seeds. Ablations vary the defense components, remove one attack component at a time, and sweep the retrieval breadth $k\in\{2,4,8\}$ and the grounding strength $\kappa\in\{0.3,0.7,1.0\}$. Every ablation arm has its own placebo, and every defense variant also faces the frozen replay.

\textbf{Baselines.} We compare grounding (with microstructure proxies, without the risk layer) with four standard defenses on the same seeds. The first ignores the news and trades on the market evidence alone. A \emph{detection filter} drops retrieved documents that the detectability classifier flags, before the top-$k$ cut; its threshold is set on news dated before the evaluation window to flag 10\% or 25\% of clean text. Two \emph{robust aggregators} replace the weighted mean of per-document scores by a retrieval-weighted median or by a mean trimmed of 25\% of the weight in each tail.

\textbf{Defense-aware attackers.} The attacker can compute the same public market evidence $s_{\mathrm{mkt}}$ as the defense. Instead of keeping its first candidate that passes the filters, the \emph{evidence-aligned} attacker makes all $K$ attempts and keeps the passing candidate whose sentiment best agrees with the sign of $s_{\mathrm{mkt}}$, so that its edits look consistent with the market. The \emph{conflict-seeking} attacker keeps the one that disagrees most. Both preserve common random numbers and record their own frozen tapes.

\textbf{Price impact.} In the main experiment injected news never moves prices. In this stress test the recorded attack moves the shared price path: on day $t$ the close shifts permanently by $\gamma\,\sigma_{t-1}\sum_{d}[s(d)-s(\mathrm{parent}(d))]$, summed over the fake documents published that day. Here $s$ is the lexicon sentiment, a stand-in for the market's reading of the text, and $\sigma_{t-1}$ is the trailing 20-day volatility of daily returns. Both twins trade the moved path and only the treated agent reads the fake text. We replay the frozen attack with $\gamma\in\{0,1,2\}$; at $\gamma=1$ the median attacked day moves by \val{ImpShiftMedian}$\,\sigma$ and the 90th percentile by \val{ImpShiftPNinety}$\,\sigma$.

\textbf{Local LLM.} We replicate the main experiment with \texttt{\val{LlmrModel}}~\cite{qwen2024qwen25}, served on the laptop by Ollama, so no data leaves the machine. It scores every distinct retrieved text, \val{LlmrTextsScored} over \val{NseedsLlmr} seeds and all 18 arms, with no parse failures.

\textbf{Real prices.} We also run the main experiment on daily BTC-USD prices from September 2014 to October 2026, the asset AutoRedTrader uses. Each of \val{NseedsReal} seeds takes its own 400-day window, chosen so that the 60-day evaluation segments do not overlap. News is generated for each window as before and still concerns the fictitious company, so no generated text names a real asset. The analog scale ($0.03$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:smkt}) becomes the median absolute 5-day move before the evaluation window. The lexicon experiments take about 7.5 hours on one laptop CPU core; the LLM reader takes 8.3 hours on an Apple M2.
