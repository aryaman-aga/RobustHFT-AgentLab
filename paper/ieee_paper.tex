\begin{abstract}
Large-language-model (LLM) trading agents fuse numerical market data
with unstructured text signals, making them vulnerable to subtle financial
misinformation: small edits to sentiment polarity, numeric magnitudes,
causal relationships or entity names that remain semantically close to
genuine news yet alter the agent's trading decision.
AutoRedTrader \cite{liu2026autoredtrader} formalised this threat as a
constrained, closed-loop red-teaming loop and demonstrated attack success
rates (ASR) of up to 26.7\% on real BTC data.
We present \rhal{}, an offline, deterministic, CPU-only framework that
reproduces the AutoRedTrader red-teaming setting without paid APIs and
extends it along four axes:
(i)~\emph{microstructure-augmented temporal grounding}, adding six
HFT-inspired proxies (range-based spread, Garman--Klass realised
variance, order-flow imbalance, volume shock, Amihud illiquidity,
range ratio) to the historical-analog evidence signal;
(ii)~an \emph{explicit risk-control layer} comprising a confidence gate,
a hold-on-conflict rule, volatility-targeted position sizing, stop-loss/
take-profit triggers, and a drawdown throttle;
(iii)~\emph{structural no-lookahead guarantees}, including a strict analog
admissibility rule ($e{+}H{\le}t$) verified by future-mutation unit tests;
and (iv)~a \emph{placebo-controlled attack metric} in which meaning-preserving
paraphrases quantify the retrieval-noise floor, yielding a
\emph{net ASR} that isolates semantic manipulation from pool-reshuffling
artefacts.
Across five seeds of fully synthetic market data,
the undefended agent achieves ASR\,${=}$\,31.7\% under attack
but the placebo arm already flips 13.5\% of decisions,
giving a net ASR of only 18.2\%.
Temporal grounding with microstructure evidence reduces naive ASR by
${\approx}$9 percentage points; the risk layer further cuts
attack-period maximum drawdown by 4 percentage points at a
quantified cost in clean cumulative return.
All code, data generators, experiments and tests are deterministic and
fully reproducible from a single integer seed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The adoption of large language models (LLMs) in quantitative finance has
accelerated rapidly.
Agents such as FinMem \cite{yu2025finmem}, FinCon \cite{yu2024fincon},
TradingAgents \cite{xiao2025tradingagents} and FinAgent \cite{zhang2024finagent}
retrieve news from a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) memory store,
reason over it jointly with price observations, and emit sequential
Buy/Sell/Hold decisions.
This architecture exposes a novel attack surface: an adversary who can insert
subtly modified news articles into the agent's retrieval pool can alter its
decisions without triggering obvious safety filters or jailbreak detectors,
because the perturbed text remains topically and syntactically plausible.

TradeTrap \cite{yan2025tradetrap} showed empirically that small textual
perturbations shift LLM-based trading decisions substantially.
AutoRedTrader \cite{liu2026autoredtrader} formalised this threat as an
autonomous red-teaming loop, \misgen{}, that (a) decomposes the attack
space into behavioural bias manipulation, minor semantic perturbations
(eight sub-types), and style rewriting, (b) uses similarity and
detectability filters to keep generated text plausible and stealthy, and
(c) adapts the operator mixture online with a P\'olya-urn sampler driven
by the agent's own decision changes.
Their reported ASR of ${\approx}26.7\%$ on a real BTC/news dataset
validates the practical threat.

Three gaps motivate \rhal{}.
\emph{Reproducibility}: the original evaluation requires paid LLM calls,
a live news feed and FAISS; a researcher cannot replicate it on a laptop.
\emph{Incomplete evidence}: the grounding signal in AutoRedTrader uses six
daily price indicators; market microstructure offers faster-moving evidence
about whether a textual claim is consistent with what traders are actually
doing in real time.
\emph{Metric validity}: ASR compares an injected run with a clean run, but
adding \emph{any} documents to a RAG pool reshuffles top-$k$ results and
flips near-threshold decisions even without semantic manipulation.
Without a paraphrase control, ASR conflates attack meaning with retrieval noise.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A compact, fully tested offline reimplementation of the
        AutoRedTrader loop that runs in mock mode without APIs and
        optionally with any OpenAI-compatible or Anthropic model.
  \item Six microstructure proxies integrated as grounding evidence,
        individually ablated to show their marginal value.
  \item An explicit post-policy risk layer with five configurable controls,
        all individually ablated.
  \item Structural no-lookahead guarantees (trailing normalisation,
        next-open execution, $e{+}H{\le}t$ analog admissibility)
        verified by future-mutation tests.
  \item A placebo arm and net ASR metric that separate semantic attack
        effect from retrieval-noise artefacts.
  \item A robustness--return frontier parameterised by grounding strength
        $\kappa$, quantifying the cost of defence in clean return.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{LLM trading agents.}
FinMem \cite{yu2025finmem} introduced layered memory and character design
for sequential trading.
InvestorBench \cite{li2025investorbench} standardised the
Perception/Profile/Memory/Action (PPMA) agent interface used in this work.
FinCon \cite{yu2024fincon} and TradingAgents \cite{xiao2025tradingagents}
extend to multi-agent and tool-augmented reasoning.
None treat adversarial text as part of their core evaluation.

\textbf{Adversarial robustness of financial agents.}
TradeTrap \cite{yan2025tradetrap} demonstrated that modest textual shifts
substantially change LLM trading decisions.
AutoRedTrader \cite{liu2026autoredtrader} is the first autonomous red-teaming
loop specialised to this setting and serves as our base.

\textbf{Adversarial NLP and red teaming.}
AutoRedTeamer \cite{zhou2025autoredteamer} and ArtPrompt \cite{jiang2024artprompt}
target general LLM safety via iterative prompt optimisation.
LIFE \cite{wang2026life} focuses on detecting LLM-generated fake news using
prompt-induced linguistic fingerprints.
GLISTERS \cite{jiang2026glisters} introduced reference-free counterfactual
financial misinformation detection, motivating the need for harder attacks.

\textbf{Behavioural finance.}
The five bias types we implement---overconfidence \cite{barber2001boys},
loss aversion \cite{kahneman2013prospect}, herding \cite{spyrou2013herding},
anchoring, and confirmation---are grounded in empirical finance literature.
Our lexicon scorer follows the spirit of Loughran--McDonald
\cite{loughran2011liability}.

\textbf{Market microstructure and backtest validity.}
We adopt the range-based spread proxy of Corwin \& Schultz
\cite{corwin2012spread}, the Garman--Klass (GK) variance estimator
\cite{garman1980estimation}, the Amihud illiquidity ratio
\cite{amihud2002illiquidity}, and the OFI formulation of Cont et al.\
\cite{cont2014price}.
Lookahead prevention follows L\'opez de Prado \cite{lopezdeprado2018advances}.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Setting}

Let $\mathcal{N}$ be a daily news corpus for a single asset and
$\text{FinAgent}$ a POMDP agent that at each day $t$ retrieves
$\mathcal{N}_t^{\text{ret}} \subset \mathcal{N}$ and chooses
$a_t \in \{\text{Buy}, \text{Sell}, \text{Hold}\}$.
An attacker controls \misgen{}, which maps clean items $n \in \mathcal{N}$
to synthetic variants $\tilde{n}$, constrained by:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A}_{\text{sim}}(\tilde{n}, n) \ge \tau_{\text{sim}}, \quad
  \mathcal{A}_{\text{det}}(\tilde{n}) \le \tau_{\text{det}},
  \label{eq:constraints}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_{\text{sim}}$ is TF-IDF cosine similarity and
$\mathcal{A}_{\text{det}}$ is a trained detectability classifier.
The attacker maximises expected decision change (ASR).
The defender modifies the agent to reduce ASR without discarding
genuinely useful textual evidence.

\subsection{Misinformation Generation (\misgen{})}\label{sec:misgen}

\misgen{} decomposes the attack into three operator sub-spaces.

\textbf{Behavioural bias ($\Phi_{\text{Bias}}$).}
One of five biases is injected into the agent's system-prompt context per day
with probability $p$: overconfidence (action threshold scaled by $0.55$),
loss aversion (negative signals up-weighted by $1.7$), anchoring
(0.5 blend with previous signal), herding (pull toward recent majority action),
and confirmation (up-weight agreeing documents).

\textbf{Minor semantic perturbations ($\Phi_{\text{Minor}}$).}
Eight sub-types operate on news item text:
\emph{flipping} (antonym substitution over directional words and causal
connectors), \emph{sentiment shift} (intensify or dampen polarity),
\emph{numerical} (scale percentages to $[1.5\times, 2\times]$ or
$<0.5\times$ preserving sign), \emph{temporal} (advance or retard
quarter/year references), \emph{entity} (swap company to competitor,
subsidiary or parent), \emph{concept} (interchange revenue/net income,
profit/loss), \emph{causal} (swap causes across polarity pools), and
\emph{other} (vague attribution phrases).

\textbf{Style rewriting ($\Phi_{\text{Rewrite}}$).}
Two rewrite styles---\emph{news} (removes attribution markers and
intensifiers, merges sentences) and \emph{academic} (formal synonyms,
intensifiers converted to hedges)---launder surface features without
altering directional words, numbers, entities or causes.
Rewriting is a fallback when a minor-perturbed candidate fails the
detectability filter.

\textbf{Placebo operator (ours).}
Synonym and discourse-connector substitution only, with no change to
sentiment polarity, direction words, numbers, entities or causes.
Used as a meaning-preserving control to establish the retrieval-noise floor.

\textbf{Filtering.}
$\mathcal{A}_{\text{sim}}$ uses TF-IDF (1--2-gram) cosine similarity with
IDF fitted once on the clean corpus.
$\mathcal{A}_{\text{det}}$ is a logistic regression over 18 interpretable
cues (polarity densities, mixed polarity, intensifier/hedge rates, numeric
magnitudes, cause--outcome mismatch, sentence structure, vague attribution,
entity count) trained on clean vs.\ \emph{minor-perturbed} text only, so
$\Phi_{\text{Rewrite}}$ can reduce detectability below $\tau_{\text{det}}$.
Threshold $\tau_{\text{det}}$ is calibrated to a 10\% false-positive rate
on held-out clean text.

\textbf{Adaptive feedback (P\'olya urn).}
Strategy weights follow \cite{liu2026autoredtrader} Eqs.~(16)--(18):
$w_s = 1 + \alpha h_s$, $P(s) = w_s / \sum w$, with a return-dependent
boost $\delta$ toward overconfidence when CR\,$>\!1$ and toward loss
aversion when CR\,$<\!1$.
After each day, if the treated agent's action differs from the shadow agent's,
the active bias and the strategies of every retrieved synthetic document are
reinforced (Eq.~(7) of \cite{liu2026autoredtrader}).
Algorithm~\ref{alg:misgen} summarises the full generation loop.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\misgen{} generation for one trading day $t$}
\label{alg:misgen}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require clean items $\{n_i\}$, history $h$, budget $K$, CR
\State $b \leftarrow$ SampleBias$(h, \text{CR})$ with prob.\ $p$
\State Inject BiasConfig $b$ into treated agent Profile
\For{each $n_i$}
  \State Sample minor type $m \sim P(s|h)$
  \State $\tilde{n} \leftarrow \Phi_m(n_i)$
  \If{Eq.~\eqref{eq:constraints} holds}
    \State Accept $\tilde{n}$; \textbf{continue}
  \ElsIf{$\mathcal{A}_{\text{sim}} \ge \tau_{\text{sim}}$}
    \State Sample rewrite $r$; $\tilde{n} \leftarrow \Phi_r(\tilde{n})$
    \If{$\mathcal{A}_{\text{det}}(\tilde{n}) \le \tau_{\text{det}}$}
      \State Accept $\tilde{n}$; \textbf{continue}
    \EndIf
  \EndIf
  \State Retry up to $K$ times; discard if all fail
\EndFor
\State Add accepted items to treated pool (\texttt{provenance="synthetic"})
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Temporal Grounding with Microstructure Evidence}
\label{sec:grounding}

\textbf{Core features} (after \cite{liu2026autoredtrader} Eq.~(19)):
1-day log return; 5-day realised volatility $\sigma_5$; 20-day drawdown;
short/long trend divergence; trailing-residual anomaly $z$-score
(an online-safe substitute for STL \cite{lopezdeprado2018advances});
and 5/20-day momentum.

\textbf{Microstructure proxies (our extension):}
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{spread}   &= (H_t - L_t) / C_t \\
  \mathrm{GK\text{-}RV} &= \tfrac{1}{2}[\ln(H_t/L_t)]^2
                    - (2\ln 2{-}1)[\ln(C_t/O_t)]^2 \\
  \mathrm{OFI}      &= (C_t - O_t) / (H_t - L_t + \epsilon) \\
  \mathrm{vol\text{-}shock} &= \ln(V_t / \bar{V}_{20}) \\
  \mathrm{illiq}    &= |r_t| / (C_t \cdot V_t) \times 10^9 \\
  \mathrm{range\text{-}ratio} &= (H_t{-}L_t) / \overline{(H{-}L)}_{20}
\end{align}
where $O, H, L, C, V$ are daily OHLCV.
GK-RV \cite{garman1980estimation} and spread \cite{corwin2012spread}
are trailed over 5-day windows; illiquidity follows Amihud
\cite{amihud2002illiquidity}; OFI follows \cite{cont2014price}.

All features are $z$-scored with a trailing 60-day window.
A bounded market evidence signal uses fixed, documented weights
(no fitting, hence no leakage):
\begin{equation}
  s_{\mathrm{mkt}} = \tanh(\mathbf{w}^{\top} \mathbf{z}_t),
\end{equation}
with $w_{\mathrm{mom}_5}{=}0.35$, $w_{\mathrm{div}}{=}0.35$,
$w_{\mathrm{OFI}}{=}0.30$, $w_{\mathrm{spread}}{=}{-}0.10$,
$w_{\mathrm{GK\text{-}RV}}{=}{-}0.10$, and smaller weights elsewhere.

\textbf{Historical analogs.}
The top-$k$ cosine neighbours of the flattened 30-day $z$-score window
at $t$ are retrieved from windows ending at index $e$, subject to
$e + H \le t$ (strict admissibility: the $H$-day forward outcome must be
fully observed before use).
Their mean forward return is blended into $s_{\mathrm{mkt}}$ via a
$\tanh$-arctanh blend with weight 0.3.

\textbf{Consistency and grounding.}
Text--market consistency is:
\begin{equation}
  c = 1 - |s_{\mathrm{text}} - s_{\mathrm{mkt}}| / 2 \;\in [0,1].
\end{equation}
Grounding shrinks inconsistent text toward market evidence with
strength $\kappa \in [0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
  s_g = s - \kappa (1 - c)(s - s_{\mathrm{mkt}}),
  \label{eq:grounding}
\end{equation}
leaving consistent text ($c \approx 1$) unchanged.

\subsection{Risk-Control Layer}\label{sec:risk}

The risk layer runs \emph{after} the policy and can only reduce or exit
positions; it never initiates a trade.
Five sequential controls are applied:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stop-loss/take-profit}: exit any open long if unrealised
        P\&L $\le -6\%$ or $\ge 12\%$.
  \item \textbf{Confidence gate}: override to Hold if confidence $< 0.40$.
  \item \textbf{Hold-on-conflict}: override to Hold if $c < 0.6$.
  \item \textbf{Vol-targeted sizing}: target position
        $= \min(1, \sigma^* / \sigma_5)$, $\sigma^* = 0.20$ (annualised).
  \item \textbf{Drawdown throttle}: halve size if portfolio drawdown $> 10\%$.
\end{enumerate}
Every override is logged for attribution in evaluation.

\subsection{Metrics}

MER and ASR follow Eqs.~(8)--(9) of \cite{liu2026autoredtrader}.
We add: \textbf{MER lift} $= \mathrm{MER}/\bar{\rho}$ (retrieval above
chance); \textbf{net ASR} $= \mathrm{ASR}_{\mathrm{inj}} -
\mathrm{ASR}_{\mathrm{placebo}}$, computed per seed;
and \textbf{active-flip ASR} (flips where the treated agent actively
trades rather than holding defensively).
We also report signal deviation, cumulative return (CR), maximum drawdown,
turnover and risk-override counts.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Data.}
We generate 500 business days of OHLCV from a 3-regime Markov drift with
GARCH(1,1) volatility \cite{bollerslev1986garch} and 3 template news items
per day with latent sentiment correlated ($\rho{=}0.35$) with the next-day
return (news is genuinely but noisily informative).
The last 60 days form the evaluation window; the preceding 440 days serve
as warm-up and analog library.
Real OHLCV can be loaded from CSV or \texttt{yfinance}.

\textbf{Arms.}
Three defence levels---\emph{none}, \emph{grounded}
($\kappa{=}0.7$, microstructure on), \emph{grounded+risk}---each run
three arms: \emph{clean}, \emph{placebo}, \emph{injected}; 5 seeds each.
\misgen{} parameters: $p{=}0.7$, $\alpha{=}1.0$, $\delta{=}0.15$,
$K{=}3$, $\tau_{\mathrm{sim}}{=}0.35$, injection ratio 1:1; TF-IDF
top-4 retrieval with 3-day recency decay.

\textbf{Ablations.}
Attack-side: remove filtering, feedback, bias, minor perturbations, or
rewriting individually.
Defence-side: none; grounding only (no micro); grounding + micro;
risk only; full; full without hold-on-conflict.
Retrieval breadth $k \in \{2, 4, 8\}$; grounding strength $\kappa \in
\{0.3, 0.7, 1.0\}$.

\textbf{Compute.}
Single-process on a laptop CPU. 45 paired runs over 5 seeds complete in
${\approx}$60\,s; 61 unit tests in ${\approx}$40\,s.

\textbf{Attack effectiveness.}
Against the undefended agent the injected arm achieves
MER\,${=}$\,67.2\% (lift $1.38\times$: rewritten documents acquire ticker
mentions, making them more retrievable than chance) and
ASR\,${=}$\,31.7$\pm$3.8\%---consistent with the ${\approx}$26.7\%
reported in \cite{liu2026autoredtrader}.
Signal deviation (0.19) is substantially higher than for the placebo
arm (0.04), confirming that misinformation moves the internal signal.

\textbf{The placebo control.}
The placebo arm flips 13.5\% of decisions despite zero directional
manipulation.
Inspection shows two causes: adding $\approx\!3$ documents doubles the
pool, displacing existing documents from the top-4 set; and $\approx\!27\%$
of clean signals lie within 0.05 of the action threshold, making them
intrinsically jittery.
Net ASR of 18.2\% is therefore the semantic effect of misinformation;
naive ASR overstates it by $1.75\times$.

\textbf{Defences.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} summarises results.
Grounding with microstructure evidence reduces naive ASR from 31.7\% to
22.4\% ($-9.3$\,pp; net ASR 9.6\%).
Adding the risk layer raises naive ASR slightly to 24.3\% because
\texttt{hold\_on\_conflict} fires whenever injected text conflicts with
market evidence and a defensive Hold is scored as a decision change by
Eq.~(9) of \cite{liu2026autoredtrader}.
Active-flip ASR falls from 8.3\% (no defence) to 5.1\% (full defence).
Attack-period maximum drawdown falls from 5.9\% to 3.9\%, which is the
risk layer's primary contribution.
Clean CR falls from 14.6\% to 10.8\% under the full defence because the
synthetic news is genuinely informative and grounding discounts it when
it disagrees with lagging price evidence.

\textbf{Attack components (Table~\ref{tab:abl_attack}).}
Removing minor perturbation collapses net ASR to 3.6\%, confirming that
\emph{semantic content} drives the attack.
Removing rewriting raises net ASR to 22.8\% while lowering MER:
without laundering, only candidates that pass the detector directly are
accepted, dominated by the decision-potent types (flipping, sentiment,
numerical); rewriting admits more detectable but decision-weak types
(entity, concept, causal) and replaces intensifiers with hedges, diluting
the lexicon signal.
Style rewriting therefore trades decision impact for retrievability and
stealth---a divergence from \cite{liu2026autoredtrader} where LLM rewrites
better preserve persuasive force.
Removing filtering, feedback, or bias changes ASR by less than 1.9\,pp
($<$1\,std): the P\'olya-urn reinforcement receives mostly noise on a
surrogate agent with a jittery action threshold, making uniform sampling
competitive.

\textbf{Defence components (Table~\ref{tab:abl_defense}).}
Price-only grounding barely helps ($-$1.9\,pp naive ASR vs.\ no defence),
because the core price features provide a coarse market state estimate.
Adding the six microstructure proxies drives the gain to $-$9.3\,pp naive
ASR ($-$8.6\,pp net), because OFI and spread give a sharper instantaneous
view of trading activity against which inconsistent text is more reliably
discounted.
The risk layer alone raises naive ASR through defensive Holds but cuts
active-flip ASR from 8.3\% to 5.8\% and MaxDD from 5.9\% to 4.1\%.
Disabling hold-on-conflict changes ASR by $<$1\,pp; the risk layer's main
contribution is to drawdown control rather than decision robustness.

\textbf{Retrieval breadth.}
Net ASR is flat for $k \in \{2,4,8\}$ (range: 17.1\%--19.4\%), confirming
the net metric is not an artefact of a particular $k$.

\section{Safety and Ethics}

\rhal{} generates synthetic financial misinformation solely to measure and
improve defensive agent robustness.
Every generated record carries a mandatory \texttt{provenance="synthetic"}
label and non-empty safety string.
Generated text is confined to in-process memory and git-ignored output
directories; no code routes orders to a broker or disseminates content
publicly.
The attacker maximises decision change, not profit; no code steers prices.
Synthetic entities are fictitious.
We follow the dual-use discussion of \cite{liu2026autoredtrader} and
release the code to support defensive research.

\section{Limitations}

(1)~The default agent is a transparent surrogate; the harness supports
real LLM mode but headline numbers are surrogate-based.
(2)~Data and news are synthetic; template news is more regular than real
reporting.
(3)~The detectability proxy (${\approx}$66\% accuracy) is a feature
classifier, not an LLM judge.
(4)~Stop-loss/take-profit checks are at daily closes, not intraday.
(5)~Sixty evaluation days per seed make CR noisy; we report paired
differences and seed variance but not formal hypothesis tests.
(6)~A threshold-based action policy is intrinsically jittery;
smoother policies (hysteresis, probabilistic sizing) would reduce
both naive and placebo ASR.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented \rhal{}, an offline, deterministic laboratory for
stress-testing LLM-based trading agents under subtle financial
misinformation.
Four main findings emerge:
(1)~the undefended agent is measurably vulnerable to semantic misinformation
(net ASR 18.2\%);
(2)~roughly half of naive ASR is attributable to retrieval-pool reshuffling
rather than misinformation semantics, making a placebo control essential;
(3)~microstructure evidence is the key component that makes temporal
grounding effective---price-only grounding adds little;
(4)~the risk layer protects drawdown and active-flip rates but its defensive
Holds inflate naive ASR, revealing a scoring artefact in the standard
ASR formula.
With rule-based operators, semantic minor perturbations drive the attack
while style rewriting weakens it, a divergence from the LLM-driven
original that the framework makes straightforward to verify with a real model.

Future work will run the same harness with real LLM policies and real price
series, introduce smoother action policies to reduce the placebo noise floor,
and study profit-seeking attackers under the same safety boundary.
